Watch a dispatcher for an hour and you will see the same eight facts typed over and over: shipper, consignee, dates, equipment, rate, reference numbers. Into the TMS. Into a rate confirmation. Into an email. Into a spreadsheet somebody keeps because the TMS does not do the thing they need.

Nobody planned this. It accumulated. And because it accumulated one form at a time, it never looks like it is worth fixing — until you count it.

## Count it once, honestly

Take one dispatcher, one week. How many minutes go into typing something the office already knows? Most freight offices we have looked at land somewhere between five and twelve hours a week across the team, and almost none of it is the work anyone was hired to do.

That is the tax. It is paid per load, forever, and it does not appear on any invoice.

## The expensive part is not the typing

It is what the typing crowds out. A dispatcher retyping a rate confirmation is not answering the broker who is deciding right now which carrier to call. The cost is the load you did not get, not the fifteen minutes.

There is a second cost that shows up later. Every retype is a chance for a number to change between documents, and a wrong reference number is a detention argument three weeks after anyone remembers the load.

## What actually fixes it

Not a new TMS. Replacing a system your people know is expensive, slow, and usually solves a different problem than the one you have.

The fix is narrower: pick the two documents that consume the most typing, and make them fill themselves from the record that already exists. That is a small piece of work with a boundary you can see from the outside — which is also why it is a sensible first project with any vendor, including us. You find out how they work before you have bet anything on them.

## The phone is the other half

The office line rings after hours and on weekends because freight does. A driver with a question at 2 AM gets a voicemail, and a broker checking a rate at 7 PM calls the next carrier on the list.

That is the same problem in a different shape: routine questions arriving when the people who can answer them are asleep. Both are fixable, and the phone is usually the one that pays first, because it is not saving you minutes — it is answering someone who was ready to give you a load.

## Where to start

Pick the single most-retyped document in your office. If you cannot name it in ten seconds, ask a dispatcher; they will name it immediately.
