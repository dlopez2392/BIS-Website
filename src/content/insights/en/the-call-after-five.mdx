In August in the Valley, an air conditioner does not fail at 10 in the morning when the office is staffed. It fails at 6:40 in the evening, when the house is 90 degrees and the owner has a free hour to deal with it. The same is true of a toothache, a truck that will not start, or a pipe under the sink. The calls that matter most tend to arrive after you have gone home.

That caller is not loyal yet. They are working down a list of search results, and the first business that gives them a real answer usually gets the job. In the Valley, a good share of them will start the conversation in Spanish.

## Four ways to handle it

Every business already has an after-hours plan, even if the plan is "nothing". These are the four we see, from least to most effort.

- **Voicemail.** Free, and fine for calls that can wait. But many callers hang up rather than leave a message, and the ones who do leave one have often called someone else by the time you hear it.
- **Your own cell phone.** Fast and personal. It also means you never really close, and the call you miss at your kid's game is the one that went to a competitor.
- **An answering service.** A person takes a message, sometimes in both languages. Better than voicemail, though the message still waits for the morning, and they cannot book a job.
- **An AI receptionist.** Answers every call in English or Spanish, takes the details, books a time on your real calendar and sends the caller a confirmation. It needs setting up properly, and it should hand anything urgent to a person.

## How to tell which one fits

Ask three questions about your last month of calls.

1. **How many came in after hours?** Your phone bill or your carrier's call log will tell you. If the answer is a handful, voicemail may be enough.
2. **What was each one worth?** A missed $90 service call is a nuisance. A missed new customer who would have come back every summer is a different number.
3. **Could the caller have booked themselves?** If most after-hours calls end with "when can you come?", then answering them is really a scheduling problem, and scheduling is the part software does well.

[The missed-calls calculator on our home page](/en#missed-calls) does the arithmetic with your own numbers in about a minute.

## What we would not do

We would not put a phone tree in front of a worried customer at 7 PM, and we would not let any system, ours included, promise a technician it cannot send. Whatever answers your phone after hours should take the details, book what can be booked, and tell the caller plainly what happens next.
