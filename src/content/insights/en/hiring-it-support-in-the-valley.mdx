In the Valley, most small businesses find their IT help the same way they find a plumber: somebody's cousin, or the person who set up the network for the shop next door. That can work out well. It can also mean that the only person who knows the Wi-Fi password, the email admin login and where the backups go is someone you cannot reach on a Saturday.

You do not need to understand the technology to judge an IT provider. You need to ask the right questions before you sign, and listen for plain answers.

## The seven questions

1. **"Who holds the passwords to my systems, and can I have them?"** The right answer is that you own every account, in your business's name, and they work under their own login that you can switch off. If the answer is vague, that is the answer. ([Why this one comes first](/en/insights/who-holds-the-keys).)
2. **"Where are my backups, and when did you last restore one?"** A backup nobody has tested is a hope. Ask them to show you a restore, of a real file, before you need one.
3. **"What happens if you are sick, on vacation or gone?"** One-person shops are common here and often excellent. They should still have a written handoff: what is installed, where, and how to get in.
4. **"How fast do you answer, and how do I reach you?"** Ask for the actual hours and the actual phone number, and whether that number is answered in Spanish if your staff need it to be.
5. **"What exactly is included each month?"** Updates, antivirus, backups, email, help for staff, new computers set up. Get it as a list. "Everything" usually turns out to mean less.
6. **"What will you not touch?"** Specialised systems such as a dental imaging machine, a point of sale or a carrier's dispatch software often belong to their own vendor. A good provider tells you where their responsibility ends, so nothing falls in between.
7. **"Can I leave, and what do I get when I do?"** You should get your passwords, your data and the written notes, with no fee for releasing them.

## Questions about AI, too

Any provider will now offer AI features. Two extra questions cover most of the risk: **which company actually processes my data**, by name, and **can I see it running somewhere** before I pay for it? If they cannot name the company or show it working, wait.
